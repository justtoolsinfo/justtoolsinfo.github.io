The sequence

How to make an exam seating arrangement

  1. 1

    Add your students

    Create one group for each class, section or subject in the session. Type roll-number ranges such as 1-40, or choose Paste a list for roll numbers with names.

  2. 2

    Add your rooms

    For each room enter the rows of desks, the desks across and the seats on each desk. The number of seats is shown as you type.

  3. 3

    Choose how to mix

    Keep Side by side and front–back for a checkerboard, or pick another rule. Choose whether seats are numbered down each column or along each row.

  4. 4

    Check the plan

    Read the message above the chart: it says whether everyone has a seat and whether any classmates still sit together. Tap Mark seats not in use for broken desks.

  5. 5

    Print

    Print the seating charts, attendance sheets, notice-board list and desk slips, or download the plan as CSV.

How the seats are filled

Seats are numbered along a path — down each column of seats, or along each row — and filled in that order. At every seat the planner takes the next student from the group that clashes least with the neighbours already seated, and prefers the group with the most students still waiting, so the groups run out together.

Clashes are ranked: two classmates on the same desk are avoided first, then side by side across an aisle, then front and back, then diagonally. Within a group, students keep the order you entered, so roll numbers still run in sequence along the path and are easy to find.

A second pass swaps seats where that removes a clash that was left over. If a clash cannot be avoided — for example when one class is more than half the room — the planner tells you how many pairs remain instead of hiding them.

What to print

  • Seating charts — the room seen from above, with every roll number in its seat. One page per room, for the door and the invigilator. Wide rooms print in landscape.
  • Attendance sheets — seat, roll number, name, group and a signature box for each student, room by room.
  • Notice-board list — for each class, which roll numbers go to which room, so students find their room at a glance.
  • Desk slips — a label for each seat with the roll number, room and seat number, to cut out and stick on desks.

Tips for a fair seating plan

  • Seat at least two classes or subjects in every session; a single class can only be spaced out, not mixed.
  • For a clean checkerboard no group should be more than half the seats in use. If one is, add a room so that spare seats can act as gaps.
  • Keep a few spare seats in every room for late changes, and mark broken desks before printing.
  • Use Shuffled order for class tests where friends would otherwise sit in roll-number order near each other.
  • Print the notice-board list the day before, and the charts and attendance sheets on the day.

FAQ

Questions about Exam Seating Arrangement Planner

Where does Exam Seating Arrangement Planner run?

Exam Seating Arrangement Planner runs in this tab on Toolspire. What you type or drop in is not sent to us and is not stored on a server.

How do I make a seating arrangement for an exam?

Add a group for each class with its roll numbers, add each room with its rows and desks, and the plan is made automatically. Then print the charts for the doors, the attendance sheets for invigilators and the notice-board list for students.

How do I make sure no two students of the same class sit together?

Seat two or more classes in the same room and keep the rule on Side by side and front–back. The planner alternates the classes like a checkerboard. It works fully when no class is more than half of the seats used; otherwise it shows how many pairs could not be separated.

Can I use only roll numbers, without names?

Yes. Type ranges such as 1-40, 45, 50-60 — prefixes and leading zeros are kept, so 23CS001-23CS060 works too. Names are optional and only appear if you paste a list.

Can I paste students from Excel or Google Sheets?

Yes. Choose Paste a list and paste two columns: roll number, then name. One student per line; a tab or a comma between the two is understood.

How are seat numbers given?

In the order seats are filled: down each column from the front, or along each row, starting at the front left. Switch on Zig-zag to make every other column or row run the other way.

What if a desk is broken or a seat must stay empty?

Switch on Mark seats not in use and tap the seat on the chart. It is taken out of the count and the plan rearranges itself. Tap it again to bring it back.

What happens if there are more students than seats?

Everyone who fits is seated, and the message above the chart tells you how many students of each group have no seat, so you know how many more seats to add.

Is the student list uploaded anywhere?

No. The plan is made in this tab and saved only in this tab’s local storage, so it is there when you return. Clear all removes it.
